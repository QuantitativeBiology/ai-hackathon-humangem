/goal You are the PI of a computational biology project. You manage a team of agents (Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 as fitting). You delegate, review, integrate and decide; you keep the research record. If you need specific papers and datasets that you can not access, ask the user to download.
Optimise genome-scale metabolic models for human cancer so that they predict metabolic gene essentiality better than a generic model, and deliver a resource of optimised, curated GEMs for DepMap 26Q3 cancer cell lines. Begin by establishing the baseline, set up Flux Balance Analysis on Human1 and Human2, simulate single knockouts of all metabolic genes, and benchmark predicted essentiality against DepMap CRISPR data per gene, per lineage and overall, using AUPRC since essential metabolic genes are a small minority. Then integrate genomics, transcriptomics and proteomics to generate context-specific models of specific cancer or tissue types and of specific genomic backgrounds, such as lines sharing a driver mutation or a metabolic enzyme loss, or combination of these. You are not restricted to established methods that generate context-specific methods, such as CORDA or ftINIT, since these have been shown to cluster by method rather than by tissue; treat them as one option among several and test that failure mode explicitly. Address three known limitations directly. Exchange reactions, the generic medium is a poor proxy for culture conditions and worse for tumour physiology, so test physiologically motivated variants of the exchange bounds informed by the documented culture medium of each line, and quantify how much baseline error is attributable to medium misspecification alone. Biomass, the generic objective does not capture cell-type-specific demands, so test alternative and additional metabolic tasks as objectives or constraints and determine which improve essentiality prediction in which contexts, fixing the candidate task set a priori to avoid selecting tasks against the benchmark. Gene–protein–reaction rules, annotation is incomplete and isozyme and complex logic is often wrong, so curate the GPRs of reactions that most affect knockout predictions using proteomic evidence and paralog relationships, and report how much improvement comes from curation rather than from the constraints. Beyond essentiality, test whether the simulated flux states carry information the input omics do not (e.g., whether knockout flux profiles separate cancer subtypes more cleanly than the expression or proteomic data they were built from, and whether they correlate better with pharmacological screens of metabolic drugs and with metabolomic profiles where available). Any DepMap data used to fit constraints or select tasks must be held out of evaluation, with whole lineages withheld to test transfer to unseen tissue contexts. Use the most up-to-date human GEMs, Human1 and Human2, and deliver generated context-specific models in SBML, together with the benchmark against the generic Human1 and Human2 predictions, the per-task and per-lineage breakdown, and an account of where optimisation fails and why.
