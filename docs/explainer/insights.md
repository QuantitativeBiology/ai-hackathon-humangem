# Key insights

Ranked within each group by how general they are, then by how surprising. "TPn" is turning point n in [the journey](journey.md). Each insight links to where it arose: a turning point in [the journey](journey.md) or a step in [the method](method.md). "Shown" claims cite a result file; "believed" marks interpretation.

## The half page

**Scientific**

- <a id="ins-s1"></a>**S1. The biggest fixable error in GEM essentiality prediction was the biomass reaction, not missing cell-line context.** A pool of vitamin and cofactor derivatives at coefficient 0.0012 makes every gene that synthesises them "lethal" in every line. Removing it gained +0.032 on held-out lineages; no data-integration method gained anything (results/w3/val/val_look_W3_M4_shortlist_decision.json; [TP5](journey.md#tp-5), [step 2](method.md#step-2)).
- <a id="ins-s2"></a>**S2. A line-constant model call is a bet on a gene's frequency, and the biomass made bad bets.** The 35 genes the pool forced to be lethal are essential in only 14% of test lines on average, whether they make catabolites (13%) or real cofactors such as heme and CoQ (15%); the genes P1 still calls lethal average 73.5% (../data/gene_groups.tsv). The safer variant keeps the 20 real-cofactor genes lethal; that group alone accounts for 0.020 of its 0.023 training deficit (validated deficit 0.021) (results/f2/decomposition.tsv; [epilogue](journey.md#epilogue)).
- <a id="ins-s3"></a>**S3. Most DepMap metabolic essentiality is gene identity, not cell-line metabolism.** A gene-frequency prior scores 0.90 against 0.30 for the generic model; P1 closes 8% of that gap; about 45% of essential gene × line pairs are invisible to FBA; and P1 carries no line-specific signal beyond expression (results/m6/test/test_look_T1_gates.json; results/w1/train_addressable.tsv; [TP2](journey.md#tp-2), [TP7](journey.md#tp-7)).

**Methodological**

- <a id="ins-m1"></a>**M1. With near-binary predictors, tie handling can outweigh the model.** 92–94% of genes tie at "no effect". Random tie-breaking moves AP by 0.025–0.053 per line, and breaking ties by expression is worth +0.12, more than both P1 fixes together (+0.05). Use tie-aware AP and tie-break baselines (notes/design_review_r1/statistics.md; results/w1/train_grid.tsv; [TP1](journey.md#tp-1), [step 5](method.md#step-5)).
- <a id="ins-m2"></a>**M2. Test "context" with shuffled and constant counterfactuals, not only against the default.** The documented medium beat Ham's, but a shuffled medium kept 97% of the gain and a constant medium did slightly better. That turned a "line-specific medium" story into "a realistic medium" (results/w2/train/shuffled_control.tsv; [TP6](journey.md#tp-6)).
- <a id="ins-m3"></a>**M3. Broad screens need held-out confirmation and controls fixed before looking.** Over 134 biomass and task modes, the expected best-of-many inflation (0.049) exceeded every training effect; only a validation look separated the real fix. Its control had been re-read after results were seen, and a post-hoc random-removal control (+0.062) was needed to make it defensible (notes/reviews/M4_W3_review.md; [TP5](journey.md#tp-5)).

## 1. Scientific and domain insights (the rest)

- <a id="ins-s4"></a>**S4. The medium matters generically, not per line.** Ham's hypoxanthine and thymidine hide de novo purine and pyrimidine dependencies (8 genes, essential in 44% of test lines on average). Serum carnitine, cholesterol and glutathione rescue 13 calls that are essential in 16% of lines. Physiological media (HPLM +0.012, Plasmax +0.010) were no better than the classic media the screens used (+0.016) (results/w2/README.md; ../data/gene_groups.tsv; [TP6](journey.md#tp-6), [step 3](method.md#step-3)).
- <a id="ins-s5"></a>**S5. The remaining errors are outside the model's scope.** All 20 worst test errors are housekeeping essentials (translation, N-glycosylation, ubiquitination, mRNA capping, DNA replication) whose functions Human1 does not represent or hides behind lumped OR rules. None is fixable by medium (results/f3/README.md; [epilogue](journey.md#epilogue)).
- <a id="ins-s6"></a>**S6. A newer, more heavily curated model was not better here.** Human2 was below Human1 in all 12 training lineages, on validation (−0.007) and on test (−0.008). The protocol differs from the Human2 paper's (generic model, tAP, Chronos labels) (results/w1/; [TP2](journey.md#tp-2)).
- <a id="ins-s7"></a>**S7. Paralog OR rules are dangerous near essential reactions.** Merging RNA polymerase subunits on the lumped RNA-synthesis reaction made 15 POLR genes non-lethal (−0.029); even a strict rule merged RRM2 (essential in 99.7% of lines) with RRM2B (0%) and lost accuracy (results/w5/README.md; results/f1/README.md; [TP4](journey.md#tp-4)).
- <a id="ins-s8"></a>**S8. Biomass pseudo-reactants couple pathways in hidden ways.** Removing the pool also removes ubiquinol, the only non-sterol use of mevalonate, so under serum HMGCR/MVK/PMVK/MVD become non-lethal. A vitamin-D catabolite demand is why serum cholesterol fails to rescue post-squalene sterol genes; a dihydroorotate entry is why uridine fails to rescue CAD (results/m6/scores/w3_mev.tsv; results/w3/README.md; [TP5](journey.md#tp-5), [step 2](method.md#step-2)).
- <a id="ins-s9"></a>**S9. Line-specific constraints barely move FBA knockouts.** Genomic losses in 665 lines changed only 22 knockout calls; E-Flux on P1 changed no lethal call (results/f4/README.md). Flux states from models that differ only by medium cannot carry line information (77/106 drug units had a change of exactly 0) (results/w6/; [TP8](journey.md#tp-8), [epilogue](journey.md#epilogue)).
- <a id="ins-s10"></a>**S10. GEM outputs add nothing to gene-prior machine learning** (features −0.003, stacking +0.000; autoresearch/; [TP8](journey.md#tp-8)).

## 2. Methodological insights (the rest)

- <a id="ins-m4"></a>**M4. Read every result against the strongest cheap baseline.** P1 beats the generic model (+0.050, 7/7 lineages) but loses to it with an expression tie-break (−0.071) and to the gene prior (−0.548). Without those baselines the headline would read as a far larger advance (results/m6/test/test_look_T1_gates.json; [TP2](journey.md#tp-2), [TP7](journey.md#tp-7)).
- <a id="ins-m5"></a>**M5. Decompose a gain by gene group before interpreting it.** "Removing artefacts" was the story; the decomposition showed that un-calling real-cofactor genes carried much of the gain, and that the known mevalonate error cost only 0.0014 (results/f2/decomposition.tsv; [epilogue](journey.md#epilogue)).
- <a id="ins-m6"></a>**M6. Parameter-free fixes transfer; CI width does not tell you how far.** The gain was +0.053, +0.052 and +0.050 on training, validation and test. The narrow CIs are line bootstraps conditional on the lineages; transfer rests on 7/7 lineages ([TP7](journey.md#tp-7), [results](method.md#results)).
- <a id="ins-m7"></a>**M7. A pre-registered order of confirmatory tests stops overclaiming.** The line-specificity test failed on test (−0.011), which turned later contrasts into estimates and blocked any personalisation claim ([TP7](journey.md#tp-7)).
- <a id="ins-m8"></a>**M8. Non-author recomputation catches what the author's tests miss**: a float-tie AP instability (0.018), a reversed candidate order, and an empty common-essential set that made two endpoints identical in dry runs ([process](journey.md#process)).
- <a id="ins-m9"></a>**M9. Watch for runner-up shrinkage.** An ML idea's lead over its baseline fell from +0.014 (training) to +0.0007 (validation); a pre-registered runner-up comparison caught it ([TP8](journey.md#tp-8)).
- <a id="ins-m10"></a>**M10. Clues found while debugging failures can beat the planned experiment.** The biomass idea came from an expression-pruning failure, a medium positive-control failure and a GPR review ([TP5](journey.md#tp-5)).

## 3. Engineering insights

- <a id="ins-e1"></a>**E1. Enforce leakage controls with mechanisms and live probes, not instructions.** A PreToolUse hook on raw label files, a runtime tripwire, a fold-scoped label API, hash-chained look ledgers and a 40-look cap. Every agent began with a probe that a label read is blocked. A hook edited mid-session stays inactive until reload. The tripwire did its job when an environment leak aborted a rehearsal ([TP1](journey.md#tp-1), [process](journey.md#process)).
- <a id="ins-e2"></a>**E2. Every long solver job needs a per-LP time limit, a CPU kill and a pinned clean worktree.** One GLPK stall cost 8.5 of W3's 24.5 CPU-h; a BLAS-thread incident cost about 7 CPU-h; two workstreams ran from uncommitted code and needed bitwise reproduction ([process](journey.md#process)).
- <a id="ins-e3"></a>**E3. Generate every quoted number from its source, and re-check after later commits.** A hand-written commit message said "all gates pass" for 39/40 models. REPORT.md's machine-checked numbers caught drift, and at HEAD four runtime totals already disagree because follow-up runs added rows ([discrepancies](discrepancies.md)).
- <a id="ins-e4"></a>**E4. Exact knockout shortcuts and per-medium models make medium-aware FBA cheap.** Skipping genes with no-flux or identical closures puts one sweep at 30–45 CPU-s; 1,361 lines collapse to 20 medium signatures per model, so the whole resource is 48 SBML files ([step 4](method.md#step-4)).
- <a id="ins-e5"></a>**E5. Round FBA ratios before ranking.** Unrounded ratios from identical runs moved AP by 0.018; rounding to 6 decimals made reruns bitwise identical ([step 4](method.md#step-4)).
- <a id="ins-e6"></a>**E6. Plan around usage limits and blocking questions.** About 9 h of usage-limit stalls went unrecorded; one stalled the agents that delivered the key fix for 6.6 h; a governance question idled the project for 4.4 h. Queue non-blocking work before asking, and log stalls ([process](journey.md#process)).
- <a id="ins-e7"></a>**E7. Run commits and looks from a scrubbed environment, and read a run's own output.** An exported PYTHONPATH aborted a ledger commit; a wrapper's exit status was once misread as the build's ([process](journey.md#process)).
